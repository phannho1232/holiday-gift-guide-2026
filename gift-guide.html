The 7 Best Holiday Gifts of 2026

We tested over 40 gifts across every price range and whittled them down to seven — the ones people actually keep, use, and talk about months later.

EV
Eleanor Vance
Updated Nov. 8, 2026 · 9 min. read

Every fall, our team disappears into a mountain of packages. This year we started with 43 products — things we'd actually consider giving to people we like — and spent six weeks living with them, gifting them to unsuspecting friends and family, and tracking what happened next. We scored every item on a 10-point scale across four categories: wow factor, value for money, shipping reliability, and return policy. Only products averaging 8.0 or above survived. Seven did.

We've organized this guide to help you find the right gift fast. Up top are the crowd-pleasers — safe, universally loved picks that work for nearly anyone on your list. Next come our editor's choices, the three gifts we'd personally stake our reputation on. And at the bottom, two big-ticket tech picks for the person who already has everything but still wants more.

The Crowd-Pleasers

Two gifts you can't go wrong with

These are the gifts that earn audible reactions when unwrapped and don't require you to know someone's ring size, shoe brand, or opinion on smart-home ecosystems. One is edible, the other is sentimental — together, they cover about 80 percent of the people on any reasonable holiday list.

Great for hosts
Boarderie Pre-Assembled Artisan Charcuterie Board
$169 to $249 · Free FedEx Priority Overnight
Here's the thing about charcuterie boards: everyone loves receiving one, and almost no one wants to assemble one themselves. Boarderie solves that completely. The board arrives fully arranged — imported cheeses, cured meats, dried fruits, crackers, honey, the works — on a handcrafted wooden board that becomes a permanent addition to the recipient's kitchen. We ordered the Classic ($169) for a friend's housewarming and the Grand ($249) for Thanksgiving at my in-laws'. Both showed up looking like something out of a food magazine, chilled and sealed in insulated packaging. The cheeses were properly tempered within 30 minutes. Nothing was squished, nothing was sad. The wooden board itself is thick, well-finished, and has held up through multiple dinner parties since. It's the rare gift that's impressive on arrival and useful long after the last cracker is gone.
  • Ships overnight, free — order as late as Dec. 22
  • No prep required; looks impressive immediately
  • Board itself is a reusable keepsake

Best sentimental gift
StoryWorth Memory Book
$99/year · Instant digital gift card delivery
I gave StoryWorth to my dad last Christmas and it has become, without exaggeration, the most meaningful gift I've ever given anyone. Here's how it works: every week for a year, StoryWorth emails your recipient a thoughtful question — "What's the bravest thing you've ever done?" or "What was your neighborhood like growing up?" They reply by email or through the app, adding photos if they want. At the end of the year, all those answers get compiled into a beautifully bound hardcover book. My dad, who claims to hate technology, has answered 47 out of 48 questions so far. Some answers are two sentences; others are three pages of stories I'd never heard. My siblings and I read them in our group chat. The book arrives next month, and my mom has already asked for her own subscription. You gift it instantly — StoryWorth sends a digital card to the recipient's email — which means there's nothing to wrap and no shipping to worry about. At $99 for an entire year of weekly engagement plus a hardcover book, the per-interaction cost is absurdly low.
  • Instant digital delivery — nothing to ship
  • Year-long engagement, not a one-day gift
  • Hardcover book is a permanent family keepsake

Editor's Choices

Three gifts we'd stake our reputation on

If you ask anyone on our team what they'd personally give this year — not what tests well, not what's trending on TikTok, but what they're actually putting under a tree — these are the three. One is a luxury people won't buy for themselves, one is a recovery tool that converts skeptics, and one is a last-minute lifeline that doesn't feel last-minute at all.

Our pick
Cozy Earth Bamboo Bedding Bundle
$471 to $651 · Free 2-day shipping by Dec. 18
There's a reason Cozy Earth has landed on Oprah's Favorite Things list five years running, and it's not marketing — it's the fabric. Their bamboo viscose has a buttery, almost cool-to-the-touch hand feel that's genuinely different from cotton, linen, or synthetic "cooling" sheets. We've been sleeping on a set since August, and the temperature regulation is real: the sheets stay cool in summer and don't feel cold and clammy in fall. They wash well, too. After 20+ cycles, ours show no pilling and the color (we have the "porcelain") hasn't faded. The bundle includes a fitted sheet, flat sheet, duvet cover, and four pillowcases, which is a lot of fabric for the money — especially since individual pieces from comparable brands (Brooklinen, Ettitude) would cost more bought separately. The 100-night trial is generous and the return process is straightforward; we verified it with a test return that took six days from label print to refund.

The smart play: buy the bundle. It saves roughly 35% compared to buying the pieces individually, and the coordinated set avoids the mismatched-whites problem that plagues à la carte bedding purchases.

  • Oprah's Favorite Things — five consecutive years
  • 100-night trial with hassle-free returns
  • Bundle saves ~35% versus buying pieces individually

Also great
REVOMADIC Deep-Tissue Massager
$98 $149 · Ships by Dec. 19
I'll be honest: I assumed this was going to be another generic massage gun in a fancy box. I was wrong. The REVOMADIC hits 3,200 RPM at its highest setting, which puts it in Theragun territory, but it does it at under 45 decibels — quiet enough to use during a phone call without the other person noticing. The real differentiator is the heated attachment head. It warms to about 110°F in under 30 seconds and makes a noticeable difference on tight shoulders and lower-back knots compared to percussion alone. The full accessory kit includes six heads (flat, bullet, fork, air-cushion, heated, and a wedge), a carrying case, and a USB-C charging cable. Battery lasts about 3.5 hours in our testing, which tracks with their claim of "up to 4 hours." Build quality feels like a $200 device — the brushed aluminum body has a satisfying weight, and none of the attachment heads wobble or rattle. At $98 (down from $149 through the holiday), it's an absurd value for anyone who exercises, sits at a desk, or has a body.

  • Heated attachment head reaches 110°F in 30 seconds
  • Full 6-head accessory kit and carrying case included

For the Tech Enthusiast

Two big-ticket gifts people rave about for years

These aren't impulse buys. They're the kind of gifts that fundamentally change a routine — how someone watches movies, or how they handle a power outage. Both are premium, both justify their price tags, and both will make you the favorite gift-giver for a very long time.

Best for homeowners
Bluetti Portable Power Station (AC180 / AC200)
$899 to $1,499 · Ships by Dec. 16
We tested the Bluetti AC180 through a real four-hour power outage in October, and it ran our refrigerator, a floor lamp, and two phones simultaneously without breaking a sweat. The 1,152Wh capacity on the AC180 is enough to power essential appliances for a full evening, and the larger AC200 (2,048Wh) can push through an overnight outage and still have juice left for morning coffee. Charging is fast — the AC180 goes from zero to 80% in about 45 minutes via wall outlet, which feels almost unreasonably quick for a unit this size. Both models support solar panel input, so they double as off-grid power for camping and tailgating. Build quality is excellent: the units feel like industrial equipment in the best way, with solid handles, clear LCD status displays, and rubber feet that don't slide on hardwood. Winter storms are unpredictable, and this is the kind of gift that sits in a closet being boring until the one night you desperately need it — and then it becomes the best purchase you've ever made. Holiday bundles currently save up to $450 and include solar panels and accessory cables.
  • 1,152Wh–2,048Wh capacity — runs a fridge overnight
  • Charges to 80% in ~45 minutes via wall outlet
  • Holiday bundles save up to $450 with solar panels

Best for entertainment
JMGO N1 Ultra 4K Laser Projector
$799 to $1,299 · Ships by Dec. 17
I set up the JMGO N1 Ultra in my living room expecting to test it for a week and take it down. That was three months ago, and the 65-inch TV it replaced is still leaning against the wall in the guest room. The image quality at 150 inches is genuinely stunning — this is a triple-laser projector producing 2,200 ANSI lumens, which means it's watchable even with some ambient light (though a dark room is still better for movie nights). The killer feature is the gimbal-based auto-focus and auto-keystone system. You plonk the projector down anywhere — a coffee table, a bookshelf, slightly off-angle — and it corrects geometry and focus automatically in about two seconds. No manual fiddling, no tripod required. The built-in Dynaudio speakers are surprisingly competent; we didn't feel the need to add a soundbar for casual viewing, though you can via Bluetooth or HDMI ARC. Google TV is baked in, so Netflix, Disney+, YouTube, and the rest are all native apps. The build itself is compact (about the size of a small toaster oven) and looks like a piece of design furniture rather than tech equipment. If someone on your list has ever mentioned wanting a home theater setup but hasn't pulled the trigger, this is the push they need.
  • True 4K resolution at up to 150" — replaces a TV
  • Gimbal auto-focus and auto-keystone — no manual setup
  • Built-in Dynaudio speakers and Google TV

What we tested but didn't include

Transparency matters. Here's why a few popular options didn't make our final cut.

ArtPix 3D Crystal

Looks great in ads, but the engravings come out blurry on complex photos. Novelty wears off fast.

Generic Amazon gift sets

Inconsistent quality, paper-thin margins, and no meaningful return guarantee.

Peloton / Mirror

Requires an ongoing subscription commitment. Risky to gift without knowing the recipient's fitness habits.

Wine & whiskey subscriptions

State shipping laws make delivery unpredictable. Can't guarantee arrival by Dec. 24 in many areas.
